Every case study opens with the client's problem, not the firm's solution — one paragraph a homeowner would actually recognize themselves in.†

Then the approach: the two or three real decisions that shaped the building, told plainly. Why the house turns away from the street. Why the roof does that. What the budget forced, and what it freed. This is placeholder rhythm — the real stories come from Q3's answers and a conversation with the architect about each project.†

Copy stays short. On project pages, photography carries the argument (R2) and text annotates it — never the reverse.
